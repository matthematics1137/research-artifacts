\begin{abstract}
Choosing a smaller quantized model file saves storage and can change which
layers fit on a GPU, but its accuracy must be measured. We evaluated Q4, IQ2,
and IQ1 GGUF files for two mixture-of-experts models on the same
\PfourPrimaryItemCount\ GSM8K arithmetic problems, using one llama.cpp build,
fixed generation settings, and recorded CPU/GPU placements. Mixtral-8x7B scored
\PfourMixtralHealthyAccuracyPct\%, \PfourMixtralIqTwoAccuracyPct\%, and
\PfourMixtralIqOneAccuracyPct\% at Q4, IQ2, and IQ1, whereas
Qwen3-30B-A3B scored \PfourQwenMoeHealthyAccuracyPct\%,
\PfourQwenMoeIqTwoAccuracyPct\%, and \PfourQwenMoeIqOneAccuracyPct\%.
Relative to Q4, the IQ2 and IQ1 files were
\PfourSizeReductionPct{\PfourMixtralIqTwoFileBytes}{\PfourMixtralHealthyFileBytes}\%
and
\PfourSizeReductionPct{\PfourMixtralIqOneFileBytes}{\PfourMixtralHealthyFileBytes}\%
smaller for Mixtral and
\PfourSizeReductionPct{\PfourQwenMoeIqTwoFileBytes}{\PfourQwenMoeHealthyFileBytes}\%
and
\PfourSizeReductionPct{\PfourQwenMoeIqOneFileBytes}{\PfourQwenMoeHealthyFileBytes}\%
smaller for Qwen. For Mixtral, Q4 was
\PfourMixtralIqTwoDeltaPp\ and \PfourMixtralIqOneDeltaPp\ percentage points
higher; both paired differences remained significant after Holm correction
across four comparisons. Qwen's smaller-file point estimates exceeded Q4, but
neither paired comparison was rejected after correction (both Holm
$p=\PfourQwenMoeIqTwoHolmP$). This does not establish improvement or
equivalence. The six-file benchmark documents substantially different
observed size--accuracy tradeoffs. The lower tiers are not precision-matched
between models, so the contrast cannot isolate architectural robustness.
The experiment, P4R1, is corrective and not independent: it replaces a
historical campaign with insufficient endpoint and model-identity provenance,
whose outcomes and hypothesis were already known. Only the replacement
measurements support the reported results.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

People running models locally often choose among several GGUF files for the
same model. In the two sets studied here, the Q4 file uses more storage and
memory than the IQ2 or IQ1 files, while a smaller file may fit more of the
model in limited GPU memory. That trade is worthwhile only if the smaller
deployment remains accurate enough for the intended task. File size is visible
before download; the accuracy cost is not.

A quantization-tier label cannot supply that missing answer. Labels such as
Q4, IQ2, and IQ1 name recipes, not a uniform precision for every tensor or a
portable accuracy penalty. Prior work reports both strong low-bit MoE results
and substantial variation across architectures and quantization methods
\citep{kim2023moqe,frantar2023qmoe,moefragile2024,
li2024quantmoebench}. The deployed result can also depend on the exact public
file, inference engine, generation settings, and the engine's placement of
model layers between GPU and system memory.

We measure the size--accuracy tradeoff for Mixtral-8x7B and Qwen3-30B-A3B,
using GSM8K grade-school arithmetic problems. Each of the six files answered the same
\PfourPrimaryItemCount\ items through one llama.cpp build and a fixed
generation and scoring protocol. Within each model, pairing correctness on the
same questions shows both the aggregate difference and which deployment was
correct when the other was not.

The answer differs between the two tested models. Mixtral scored
\PfourMixtralHealthyAccuracyPct\% at Q4,
\PfourMixtralIqTwoAccuracyPct\% at IQ2, and
\PfourMixtralIqOneAccuracyPct\% at IQ1; both Q4-to-low differences remained
significant after correction for four comparisons. Qwen scored
\PfourQwenMoeHealthyAccuracyPct\%, \PfourQwenMoeIqTwoAccuracyPct\%, and
\PfourQwenMoeIqOneAccuracyPct\%, respectively. Its smaller-file point
estimates were higher than Q4, although neither comparison was rejected after
Holm correction. One model's result therefore does not tell a local user what
the same tier choice will do for another model.
The smaller files also use different effective precisions: Mixtral IQ1 and
IQ2 have \PfourMixtralIqOneEffectiveBpw\ and
\PfourMixtralIqTwoEffectiveBpw\ whole-file bits per parameter, while Qwen's
corresponding files have \PfourQwenMoeIqOneEffectiveBpw\ and
\PfourQwenMoeIqTwoEffectiveBpw. We compare the available files within each
model; their labels do not define a matched cross-model intervention.

The contribution is an item-paired benchmark of six named deployments,
including the exact files, observed placement, and reproducible statistical
analysis. Section~\ref{sec:methods} explains the corrective study design and
defines the deployments. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports the paired results, and the
remaining sections explain their practical meaning, limits, and
reproducibility.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Study status and scope}

P4R1 is a corrective exploratory experiment, not independent confirmation.
A historical campaign did not preserve enough endpoint, model-identity, and
placement provenance to attribute its saved scores reliably. We replaced all
six GSM8K cells under a fixed protocol with explicit identity checks. The
historical outcomes, sampled items, and motivating hypothesis were already
known, which limits confirmatory interpretation even though the replacement
protocol was frozen before execution. Only these six replacement cells
contribute results. Historical MATH and HumanEval+ measurements are retained
as audit context and are outside the present study.

\subsection{Models and artifacts}

The base checkpoints are Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct-v0.1 and
Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507, abbreviated below as Mixtral and Qwen.
GGUF is the model container used by llama.cpp for CPU, GPU, and hybrid
inference \citep{gerganov2023llamacpp}. Labels such as \q{IQ1\_M},
\q{IQ2\_XXS}, and \q{Q4\_K\_M} name quantization recipes. They do not assert
that every stored tensor uses one, two, or four bits. We therefore characterize
each deployment by its exact file and by effective whole-file bits per stored
parameter,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{effective\ file\ bpw}
  = \frac{8\,(\mathrm{artifact\ bytes})}
           {\mathrm{parameters\ enumerated\ from\ GGUF\ tensor\ shapes}}.
\end{equation}
This measure includes tensor-specific allocations and file overhead. It does
not isolate routed-expert precision.

quantization recipe, effective precision, or expert geometry.

\subsection{Dataset, generation, and scoring}

The evaluation uses \PfourPrimaryItemCount\ examples sampled without
replacement from the GSM8K test split \citep{cobbe2021gsm8k}. The source
revision and subset digest are frozen. Sampling used Python's deterministic
random generator with seed \PfourDatasetSamplingSeed. Every cell received the
same item identifiers in the same evaluation contract.

Each request contained one user message: the GSM8K question followed by
``End your response with: Answer: <number>''. There was no separate system
message and no worked-example prompt. The serving engine applied each
artifact's chat template.

All six cells used a 4096-token context, a maximum of
\PfourPrimaryMaxTokens\ generated tokens, temperature \PfourTemperature,
top-$p$ \PfourTopP, top-$k$ \PfourTopK, and concurrency
\PfourConcurrency. The per-item sampler seed was 4,200,000 plus the integer
GSM8K item suffix. Thus each item used the same per-item seed shared across
all six artifacts. The request specified \q{\PfourReasoningEffort} reasoning
effort. No cell dropped the chat-template keyword arguments.

The scorer extracts the final numeric answer and compares it with the gold
value using absolute and relative tolerance
\PfourGsmScorerTolerance. Private outputs and gold answers were recomputed
during promotion. A request failure, malformed response, parser failure, or
missing answer was scored wrong rather than omitted. The promoted rows contain
no request errors. The output limit was reached once in Mixtral IQ1 and once
in each Qwen cell; these rows remain in their assigned cells.

\subsection{Engine, placement, and lifecycle evidence}

All cells ran through the same llama.cpp executable from clean commit
\texttt{035e2273\allowbreak 1a7fd70b\allowbreak 9854b3a2\allowbreak
d64ec68e\allowbreak 9b1a45d3}, built for CUDA 12.6 and the
target GPU architecture. The executable, build cache, compiler tools, CUDA
libraries, and driver were hashed or read back before inference. The machine
used an Intel Core i9-14900HX, \PfourRamGib\,GiB of RAM, and an NVIDIA RTX
4080 Laptop GPU with \PfourGpuVramMib\,MiB of VRAM at an
\PfourGpuPowerW\,W power limit.

Cells ran sequentially with one server at a time. Before scoring, the guard
verified the intended model hash and endpoint alias, rejected stale listeners
or foreign compute processes, and captured the engine's placement readback.
Mixtral reported \PfourMixtralHealthyGpuLayers/33,
\PfourMixtralIqTwoGpuLayers/33, and
\PfourMixtralIqOneGpuLayers/33 GPU-offloaded layers for Q4, IQ2, and IQ1.
Qwen's offloaded-layer counter was 49/49 for every tier, but it includes
partially offloaded layers: automatic fitting reported system-memory overflow
for 25 Q4 layers and two IQ2 layers. IQ1 required no fit adjustment. Thus the
18.6\,GB Q4 file was not wholly GPU-resident, and the matching counters do not
establish matched tensor placement. The artifact includes hash-bound startup
excerpts and separate CPU-mapped and CUDA model-buffer readbacks; these buffer
sizes are not additive measurements of physical resident memory.

Each cell has a startup receipt, completion receipt, and shutdown receipt; the next
server could not begin until the previous process tree and listener were gone.
Post-campaign verification rechecked model content and the CUDA runtime.

These controls address the specific failure of the historical run: P4R1 binds
the response stream to the intended endpoint, exact model artifact, observed
placement, and server lifecycle. They do not turn placement into a randomized
factor. In particular, the Mixtral artifact tiers also differ in the number of
layers placed on the GPU.

\subsection{Statistical analysis}

For each cell we report exact accuracy and a 95\% Wilson interval
\citep{wilson1927}. Within a model, Q4 and each low tier answer the same
items. Let $Y_{i,Q4}$ and $Y_{i,L}$ denote binary correctness for item $i$.
The paired estimand is
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\Delta}_{Q4-L}
  = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(Y_{i,Q4}-Y_{i,L}).
\end{equation}
Positive values mean Q4 scored higher; negative values mean the low tier
scored higher.

We report Newcombe method-10 paired 95\% intervals for
$\widehat{\Delta}_{Q4-L}$ and exact two-sided McNemar tests
\citep{newcombe1998paired,mcnemar1947}. Holm's step-down procedure controls
family-wise error across exactly four comparisons
\citep{holm1979}. The Newcombe intervals are pointwise; they are not adjusted
simultaneous intervals. The family-wise decision follows the Holm-adjusted
McNemar values. We did not predefine an equivalence margin, so failure to
reject is not an equivalence result.

\subsection{Mixtral: the smaller files scored lower}

Choosing IQ2 saved
\PfourSizeReductionPct{\PfourMixtralIqTwoFileBytes}{\PfourMixtralHealthyFileBytes}\%
of the Q4 file size and reduced observed accuracy by
\PfourMixtralIqTwoDeltaPp\ percentage points. Choosing IQ1 saved
\PfourSizeReductionPct{\PfourMixtralIqOneFileBytes}{\PfourMixtralHealthyFileBytes}\%
and reduced it by \PfourMixtralIqOneDeltaPp\ points. The paired evidence is
asymmetric: Q4 answered \PfourMixtralIqTwoHealthyOnlyCorrect\ items that
IQ2 missed, while IQ2 answered \PfourMixtralIqTwoLowOnlyCorrect\ that Q4
missed. The corresponding Q4-only and IQ1-only counts were
\PfourMixtralIqOneHealthyOnlyCorrect\ and \PfourMixtralIqOneLowOnlyCorrect.
Thus the losses concern many of the same questions, rather than a comparison
of unrelated samples.

Both exact McNemar tests reject after Holm adjustment at $\alpha=0.05$.
Table~\ref{tab:paired} reports the paired intervals and adjusted values;
the unadjusted values are $p=\PfourMixtralIqTwoMcNemarP$ for IQ2 and
$p=\PfourMixtralIqOneMcNemarP$ for IQ1. The measured storage saving came
with a substantial GSM8K accuracy cost for these Mixtral deployments.

\subsection{Qwen: the low-tier point estimates exceed Q4}

Qwen IQ2 and IQ1 saved
\PfourSizeReductionPct{\PfourQwenMoeIqTwoFileBytes}{\PfourQwenMoeHealthyFileBytes}\%
and
\PfourSizeReductionPct{\PfourQwenMoeIqOneFileBytes}{\PfourQwenMoeHealthyFileBytes}\%
of the Q4 file size. Their scores exceeded Q4's
\PfourQwenMoeHealthyCorrect/\PfourPrimaryItemCount:
\PfourQwenMoeIqTwoLowOnlyCorrect\ discordant items favored IQ2 and
\PfourQwenMoeIqOneLowOnlyCorrect\ favored IQ1, with none favoring Q4 in
either comparison. In the Q4-minus-low convention, the differences are
\PfourQwenMoeIqTwoDeltaPp\ and \PfourQwenMoeIqOneDeltaPp\ points.

intervals in Table~\ref{tab:paired} are not simultaneous intervals or

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{The practical answer}

The file-size tradeoff did not have one accuracy answer across the two tested
models. The Mixtral Q4 file answered substantially more of the same questions
correctly than either smaller file. For Qwen, both smaller-file point
estimates exceeded Q4, but the Holm procedure did not reject either comparison
at a 0.05 family-wise threshold. A local user therefore cannot transfer the
Mixtral result to Qwen, or the Qwen point estimates to another model, from
tier names alone.

This is a deployment result, not a universal MoE quantization rule or a tested
cross-model interaction. The paired tables add information that six marginal
percentages would miss.
Mixtral's Q4-only counts greatly exceed its low-only counts. The Qwen
discordances in this sample all run in the opposite observed direction. That
is why the results should be reported as signed paired outcomes. At the same
time, only five or six Qwen items were discordant, so multiplicity-aware
inference remains limited.

\subsection{Why the contrast does not identify a cause}

The effective precisions differ before architecture enters the comparison.
Qwen's IQ1 file uses \PfourQwenMoeIqOneEffectiveBpw\ whole-file bpw,
more than even Mixtral's IQ2 file at \PfourMixtralIqTwoEffectiveBpw\ bpw.
The Q4 files are close in effective bpw, but matching that anchor does not
match the amount or tensor-level distribution of compression at the lower
tiers. This alone prevents reading the two ladders as a controlled test of
low-bit robustness.

The architectures also differ in expert granularity:
\PfourMixtralExperts\ experts with \PfourMixtralActiveExperts\ active for
Mixtral, versus \PfourQwenMoeExperts\ with
\PfourQwenMoeActiveExperts\ active for Qwen. Fine-grained MoE designs are
intended to partition capacity and routing more flexibly
\citep{dai2024deepseekmoe,qwen2025qwen3}. It is plausible that expert width,
routing alternatives, or tensor allocation changes how weight error affects a
model.

P4R1 compares one checkpoint from each family. Expert count changes together
with training, model width, router design, active-expert count, quantization
recipe, and placement. A follow-up could test granularity with multiple
checkpoints and controlled precision and placement. The present observations
cannot favor that explanation over the other differences.

\subsection{Implications for local deployment}

The smaller file can ease a storage or memory constraint and may allow more
layers to be assigned to the GPU. The present Mixtral offload counters show
that the engine assigned more layers to the GPU for IQ2 and IQ1 than
for Q4. This paper measures the other half of the decision---whether the
smaller deployment still answers the target task correctly enough. It does
not combine speed and accuracy into a single recommendation.

The relevant unit is the exact artifact and engine under its actual memory
placement. Effective file bpw is more informative than a tier label, but it
still cannot predict accuracy by itself. A sound local evaluation should hash
the artifact, verify the serving endpoint and loaded model, record placement,
keep the task and generation protocol fixed, and pair responses on the same
items. This is especially important when the GPU cannot hold every artifact
under the same placement.

The result also cautions against treating Q4 as a universal quality ceiling.
It is a practical within-model anchor. P4R1 includes no unquantized or
near-lossless reference, so it cannot determine which artifact is closest to
the underlying checkpoint's maximum GSM8K accuracy.

\section{Threats to Validity}

P4R1 is not independent. The historical outcomes, exact item set, and
hypothesis were known before the corrective run. The protocol and analysis
were frozen for P4R1, but the resulting evidence is best interpreted as a
provenance-corrected exploratory measurement. An independent confirmation
would require newly sampled items, prospectively fixed hypotheses, and
preferably additional model families.

The historical campaign's endpoint, model-identity, and placement provenance
was insufficient. P4R1 repairs those links for the replacement measurements;
it cannot validate the earlier scores. Historical MATH and HumanEval+ results
remain outside this study.

The evaluation covers one task, \PfourPrimaryItemCount\ items, one seeded
decode per item and artifact, and one machine. It does not estimate
run-to-run sampling variability. A few responses reached the output limit,
although no promoted request was dropped and failures would have counted as
wrong. GSM8K numeric-answer accuracy does not measure coding, factual recall,
dialogue quality, calibration, or long-context behavior.

The comparisons are deployed configurations rather than isolated
quantization treatments. Mixtral placement changes from
\PfourMixtralHealthyGpuLayers/33 layers at Q4 to
\PfourMixtralIqTwoGpuLayers/33 and \PfourMixtralIqOneGpuLayers/33 at the
lower tiers. Qwen's matching 49/49 counters also conceal different
system-memory overflow and model-buffer allocations. Model family, artifact
recipe, and tensor allocation differ across the ladders. Effective whole-file bpw
does not reveal expert-tensor precision. Consequently, neither cross-model
differences nor within-ladder changes identify a quantization algorithm's
causal effect in isolation.

Finally, the paired intervals are pointwise while the hypothesis decisions
use Holm-adjusted $p$ values. No simultaneous confidence intervals,
equivalence margin, power target, or preregistered minimum effect of interest
was specified. The Qwen results therefore support their observed signed point
estimates and the stated family-wise non-rejections, not interchangeability
among artifacts.

\section{Related Work}

MoE architectures reduce active computation by routing tokens through a
subset of experts \citep{fedus2021switch,jiang2024mixtral}. DeepSeekMoE and
Qwen develop finer-grained expert organizations that motivate expert
granularity as a variable of interest
\citep{dai2024deepseekmoe,qwen2025qwen3}. Offloading work addresses the
systems problem of serving MoEs when all weights do not reside in fast device
memory \citep{eliseev2023offloading}.

MoQE uses expert-aware mixed precision and an optimized runtime
\citep{kim2023moqe}; QMoE co-designs a compression format and GPU decoding
kernels \citep{frantar2023qmoe}. QuantMoE-Bench studies structure-aware bit
allocation, and Q-Strata optimizes hierarchical mixed-precision allocation
across MoE blocks \citep{li2024quantmoebench,lee2026qstrata}. These are
quantization or execution methods. P4R1 introduces no corresponding optimizer,
quantizer, or kernel.

Colla-Q allocates expert precision using activation entropy and evaluates
Mixtral at average bit-widths from 1.57 to 2.54 with GPTQ-based quantization
\citep{shin2026collaq}. Its benchmark suite and bit allocation differ from
the downloadable GGUFs tested here. Such method-level results reinforce why
our Mixtral losses cannot be generalized to every low-bit representation of
that model.

Evaluations spanning models and quantizers already report substantial
variation, including low-precision fragility in Mixtral
\citep{moefragile2024,li2024quantmoebench}. A pre-existing public GGUF
Quantization Benchmark also compares tiers within a model under llama.cpp and
explicitly frames the practical question as the accuracy lost when moving to a
smaller GGUF \citep{rougy2026ggufbench}. MoE-XBench evaluates the same
Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 checkpoint in llama.cpp: its Q4\_K\_M GSM8K score
is 0.833 against 0.836 in BF16. Its low-bit sensitivity analysis uses
perplexity and excludes IQ1\_M from the primary quantization-sensitivity
aggregate; the paper also reports IQ1\_M runtime measurements
\citep{benazir2026moexbench}. It does not report the item-paired GSM8K
comparison for our six public files. Even where the checkpoint and task
overlap, its generated artifacts and evaluation protocol differ. The scores
cannot be combined with ours or treated as a matched replication. We make no
general priority claim for within-model GGUF benchmarking. The narrower
contribution here is item-paired GSM8K evidence
with artifact, engine, placement, and lifecycle provenance for these six exact
deployments; it is not an expert-count scaling law.

\section{Reproducibility and Artifact Availability}

The version-1.0.0 reproducibility compendium is identified by
\href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22346482}{doi:10.5281/zenodo.22346482};
the corresponding source and report use the
\href{https://github.com/matthematics1137/research-artifacts/tree/moe-quantization-granularity-v1.0.0/papers/2026-moe-quantization-granularity}{versioned research-artifact directory}.
It contains 600 sanitized item-level outcomes, frozen claims, exact model and
dataset identities, engine/build and placement metadata, the replication
sources, and this report. Benchmark questions and answers, response prose,
model weights, runtime binaries, and private lifecycle logs are excluded.
The evidence records bind the public projection to the checked private
campaign; the public archive does not expose the private logs themselves.

Analysis reproduction needs only Python's standard library. From the artifact
root, \q{python3 check\_claims.py} recomputes the six cell counts, four
discordance tables, Wilson and Newcombe intervals, exact McNemar values, and
Holm adjustment. The paper rebuild uses the shipped figures and cached
Tectonic 0.17.0. Full replication instead obtains the pinned upstream GSM8K
revision and six model files, verifies their hashes, and runs one server at a
time under the documented protocol. It requires compatible hardware,
84.23\,GiB of model files, and local review of memory and thermal limits.

\section{Conclusion}
\enlargethispage{\baselineskip}

Choosing a smaller GGUF file did not produce a uniform accuracy cost across
the two tested models. On the same \PfourPrimaryItemCount\ GSM8K items,
Mixtral scored \PfourMixtralHealthyAccuracyPct\% at Q4,
\PfourMixtralIqTwoAccuracyPct\% at IQ2, and
\PfourMixtralIqOneAccuracyPct\% at IQ1; both Q4-to-low paired differences
remained significant after Holm correction. Qwen scored
\PfourQwenMoeHealthyAccuracyPct\%, \PfourQwenMoeIqTwoAccuracyPct\%, and
\PfourQwenMoeIqOneAccuracyPct\%. The smaller-file point estimates were above
Q4, but neither comparison was rejected after Holm correction.
The Qwen non-rejections establish neither improvement nor equivalence.

The useful result is the measured storage--accuracy tradeoff for these exact
files on this task. Lower-tier precision differs between the model families,
and the experiment does not isolate an architectural cause. A broader
recommendation requires newly sampled tasks and items, additional
checkpoints, and matched precision and placement.
