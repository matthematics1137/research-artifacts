# P9 phase 1 registration: what sets a 2-bit matrix call's time on two consumer GPUs (2026-11-05)

Registered 2026-11-05 13:53 EST, before any measurement below runs. Gate: `research/gates/2026-11-05-p9-kernel-calls.md`.
RIFE: research-ledger-reference. Prior art: `research/raw/2026-11-05-p9-prior-art/prior-art-search.md` (partly known: the
methods are published, these measurements are not).

## 0. What is known, and three facts found while writing this

From P8 and its audit (`research/P8_AUDIT_2026-11-05.md`, sections 2.3-2.4), on the int8 one-row ("sq") kernel that runs
every one-row matrix call:
- On the 2080 Ti a 1-bit call takes 96.5-99.3% of a 2-bit call's time in the engine's censuses, and 104-124% in
  isolated benches.
- On w13's final kernels each 2-bit call has a fixed part of about 11 µs: 11.7 in the w12p census, 10.7-11.3 in the
  unprofiled bench of 1 November.
- On the laptop (stock kernels, under the profiler), the 1-bit per-weight time follows the SM clock (0.712 µs per
  million weights at 1515 MHz, 0.481 at 2310 MHz: ratio 1.48, clocks 1.52). The 2-bit per-weight time follows the
  memory clock (0.828 at 7001 MHz, 0.650 at 9001 MHz: 1.27 against 1.29).
- The account "per-weight time = max(read, decode)" is post hoc, and the cause of the fixed part is open.

Three facts found while designing this registration:
1. **The 1 November bench re-read each matrix from cache.** `tools/turing/gemv_bench.py` replays 20 calls on one
   weight tensor, so a matrix smaller than the L2 cache is served from it after the first call.
   - The 2080 Ti's L2 is 5.5 MB. It holds attn_kv at every bit width and gdn_z and gdn_out at 1 bit.
   - The laptop's L2 is 48 MB. It holds every model matrix at 2 bits.
   - The engine reads each matrix once per token, from DRAM. This registration's bench rotates through enough
     copies that the bytes replayed exceed three times the L2.
2. **On the laptop, w13 differs from stock almost only by W12p.** W9's host-chosen slice height is on only where the
   opt-in shared memory is under 80 KB (`gemv_int8_sq_fit_mode`), so it is off on Ada (99 KB). W7's fit gate does
   nothing there either, and W10, W10b, W11, W11b and W13 do not touch one-row calls on sm_89. So at one row,
   w13 on Ada is stock plus W12p's stepped row pointer. `EXL3_INT8_SQ_FIT=1` adds W9.
3. **GPU counters need root on both machines** (`RmProfilingAdminOnly: 1`). Nsight Compute runs only if Matt runs
   `tools/turing/p9_ncu.sh` with sudo (section 2, M4).

## 2. Measurements

### M1. Engine calls, unprofiled (`tools/turing/p9_bench.py`)

One activation row through `ext.exl3_gemm`, the dispatch the model uses, on synthetic trellis tensors (random words,
random ±1 input and output flips, as in `gemv_bench.py`). Cases:
- **Model shapes** at K = 1, 2, 3, 4: attn_kv 5120×1024, gdn_z 5120×6144, gdn_out 6144×5120, gdn_qkv 5120×10240, attn_q
  5120×12288, mlp_gate 5120×17408, mlp_down 17408×5120.
- **Near-empty call** at K = 1, 2: 128×256.
- **Grid** at K = 1, 2: k in {2048, 5120, 10240, 17408} × n in {1024, 4096, 10240, 17408}.
- **Fused bundles** at K = 1, 2: gate+up 5120×34816, qkv+z 5120×16384, q+k+v 5120×14336.

**Cache.** Each case allocates N copies of the weight tensor, with N the larger of 20 and three L2 sizes over its
bytes, capped at 400. One CUDA graph replays one call per copy. Replays repeat until each timing covers at least 600
calls. The per-call time is the elapsed time over the number of calls, so it includes the in-graph gap a decode step
also pays.

**Rounds.** Seven rounds. Each round runs every case once, in an order rotated by five places per round. Each case's
value is the median over rounds.

**Decomposition.** `EXL3_INT8_SQ_FIT_LOG=1` records the host's slice height, ksplit and grid for every shape and K.

**Arms:**
- 2080 Ti: w13.
- Laptop: the official wheel, stock89, w13sm89, and w13sm89 with `EXL3_INT8_SQ_FIT=1` (W9 on).

The laptop runs its arms in one session, each round cycling through the arms.

### M2. Phase split of the one-row kernel (`tools/turing/p9_phases.py`, `tools/turing/p9/phases.cu`)

A copy of `exl3_gemv_int8_sq_kernel` for one row, compiled against each build's own patched source. It has switches
for its parts:
- **stage:** each slice's input Hadamard transform, maximum and int8 quantization;
- **loop:** the per-weight main loop, the unit;
- **epilogue:** the fixed-order combine over slices, the output Hadamard transform and the scale;
- **counter:** the completion counter that gates the epilogue.

Variants:
- **full:** all four parts. It must reproduce the engine's output bit for bit at every shape and K. That match also
  proves the decomposition matches, because the per-slice scales depend on the slice height.
- **one part removed:** no stage, no loop, no epilogue.
- **one part alone, plus the counter:** loop only, stage only, epilogue only.
- **counter only.**
- **null:** a kernel that returns at once, with the same grid and shared memory.

The decomposition (slice height, grid) is the engine's own for each card: W9's host rule on the 2080 Ti, the
kernel's rule on the laptop. It is computed in Python and checked against the engine's log and the bit-identical
output. Also measured: the 1-bit loop at the 2-bit call's decomposition, and the reverse.

Coverage: K = 1, 2 on the model shapes and the grid. Copies, graphs, rounds and rotation as in M1. Run on the 2080
Ti (w13 source) and the laptop (stock89 and w13sm89 sources).

### M3. Static machine code (`tools/turing/p9_sass.py`)

`cuobjdump -sass` of each build's one-row sq kernel instances at K = 1 and 2: the official wheel's sm_75 and sm_89
code, stock89, w13 and w13sm89.
- **Loop.** The main loop is the innermost loop holding the IDP.4A instructions.
- **Counts.** Instructions per iteration by opcode, and per weight. Each IDP.4A multiplies four weights for one lane,
  so the weights per lane per iteration are four times the IDP.4A count.
- **Floors** for per-weight time:
  - the issue floor: instructions per weight over 4 warp instructions per clock per SM × 32 lanes × SMs × SM clock;
  - one floor per pipe, from the throughput table in the CUDA C++ Programming Guide for each compute capability. An
    opcode the table does not cover is listed as unassigned, not guessed.

### M4. Hardware counters (`tools/turing/p9_ncu.sh`; runs only with Matt's sudo)

Nsight Compute on the full and loop-only variants at K = 1 and 2, on two shapes (mlp_gate, gdn_qkv), on the laptop:
- issue-active fraction;
- per-pipe utilization;
- warp stall reasons: long scoreboard, wait, math pipe throttle and barrier;
- achieved DRAM bandwidth.

If it does not run, the paper says so, and the loop's limiter stays at what M2 and M3 support.

## 3. Estimands and analysis (`tools/turing/p9_analyze.py`)

- **Fit.** For each arm and K: t = a + b × weights by least squares over the seven model shapes' medians. The
  interval is a t interval (6 degrees of freedom) over the seven per-round fits. The grid fit is secondary and adds
  ksplit as a covariate.
- **Matrix excess.** Over the d0-w12p census's matrix call list (`testsuite/results/turing/census/d0-w12p/`), each
  call's fitted time minus its read at 550 GB/s. On the laptop, the read rate is 432 GB/s × memory clock / 9001 ×
  550/616, as in `tools/turing/call_split_ada.py`. The fixed share is the sum of the fixed parts over the excess.
- **Fusion saving** per merged call: (the separate calls' sum − the fused call) / (parts − 1), in units of a at the
  same K.
- **Phase contributions:** the full variant's intercept minus the intercept without the part, and the null
  kernel's intercept.
- **Cycles per weight:** c = b × SMs × median SM clock during the timings (SM-cycles per weight). The fixed part in
  cycles is a × SM clock.

## 4. Registered predictions (confidence in brackets)

**2080 Ti, w13, M1:**
- **H1** [80%]: a at K = 2 is 9-13 µs, with interval half-width at most 1.0 µs.
- **H2** [75%]: the fixed parts are at least 70% of the matrix excess.
- **H3** [85%]: the near-empty call takes at most 6 µs. The null kernel's intercept (M2) is at most 3 µs.
- **H4** [80%]: 1-bit calls are no faster than 2-bit calls. The geometric mean of the time ratios over the seven
  model shapes is 0.95-1.20.
- **H5** [65%]: fusion saves less than 0.5 a per merged call in all three bundles at K = 2. Refuted, with fusion the
  lever, if it saves at least 0.8 a in two of three.

**2080 Ti, M2:**
- **H6** [55%]: at K = 2 the main loop's contribution is at least half the full intercept, and stage plus epilogue
  together at most 40%.
- **H7** [65%]: the 1-bit penalty is not a decomposition artifact. At the 2-bit call's decomposition, the 1-bit
  loop-only time is at least 90% of the 2-bit loop-only time on the four largest model shapes.

**Static code, M3:**
- **H8** [75%]: the main loop issues at least 0.9 times as many instructions per weight at 1 bit as at 2 bits, on
  sm_75 and sm_89.
- **H9** [60%]: W12p removes at least 5% of the main loop's instructions per weight at 1 bit (stock against w13, same
  target).

**Laptop, M1 and M2:**
- **H10** [80%]: stock89 and the official wheel are within 3% on every model shape at K = 1 and 2.
- **H11** [55%]: w13sm89 against stock89 at default settings: b at K = 1 falls by at least half of H9's instruction
  reduction, and b at K = 2 is within 5%.
- **H12** [75%]: on Ada, 1 bit is faster per weight. b(K=1)/b(K=2) is at most 0.85 for stock89 and w13sm89.
- **H13** [65%]: the 1-bit cycles per weight (stock) agree within 10% across the two censuses (80 W and 175 W) and
  this bench. It is the third condition, and the first unprofiled.
- **H14** [55%]: the 2-bit fixed part follows the SM clock. a × SM clock is within 20% of the censuses' 12.7-14.9
  thousand cycles.
- **H15** [50%, descriptive]: the 1-bit cycles per weight for the same w13 source, 2080 Ti over laptop, is 0.9-1.4.
- **H16** [50%, exploratory]: W9 on (`EXL3_INT8_SQ_FIT=1`) changes the laptop's a at K = 2 by less than 1.5 µs.

**Limiter of the 1-bit main loop** (M3, and M4 if it runs). Decision rule, named in advance:
- **Issue-bound:** the issue floor is at least 80% of the measured b at K = 1, and the issue-active fraction is at
  least 70% where measured.
- **Pipe-bound:** a pipe floor is within 10% of b and above the issue floor.
- **Latency-bound:** both floors are under 60% of b, and long-scoreboard stalls lead where measured.
- **Unresolved:** anything else, named as such.

## 5. What the outcomes change

- **P8.** Its section 4.3 cites the 1 November bench (10.7-11.3 µs, 81-84%) and the 104-124% isolated ratios. If M1
  moves the fixed part by more than 1 µs, or the 1-bit ratios out of 104-124%, P8 cites this registration's result
  in one sentence before its release. P8 is unpublished, so this is a fix, not an erratum.
- **The lab's next kernel work** follows the limiter:
  - issue-bound or pipe-bound: fewer instructions per weight (W12p's direction);
  - latency-bound: more loads in flight;
  - a dominant stage or epilogue: those phases.
- **H5 refuted** puts fusion ahead of all of these.

## Amendment 1 (2026-11-05 14:19 EST, after the smoke tests and before the first non-smoke row)

The smoke tests (one round, two shapes, rows tagged `smoke`, never analysed) found three problems in the procedure.
These changes fix them. No shape, arm, threshold or prediction changes.

1. **Warm-up before every timing.** Each timing now follows 0.15 s of the same graph's replays
   (`p9_common.WARM_S`).
   - Why: the laptop's SM clock was still rising during the first minute of a process. The 1-bit loop's time fell 29%
     between the start and the end of one smoke run, while the memory-bound 2-bit loop's held.
   - The first timing of each process warms for 2 s (`p9_common.WARM_FIRST_S`). In the laptop's smoke run, the
     first timing ran at the idle clocks (210 MHz SM, 405 MHz memory), and the next three at 1665-1853 MHz.
   - Clock join: a timing's clocks are the median of the samples from 0.15 s before its start to its end, that is
     over the warm-up and the timing. Each driver records its machine's UTC offset, because nvidia-smi stamps local
     time.
2. **M2's variants hold the full kernel's residency.** Removing a part changed the compiler's register allocation. On
   sm_89, "no stage" took 60 registers against the full kernel's 48, fell from 5 to 4 blocks per SM, and ran in two
   waves at the engine's grid. Two fixes:
   - The full kernel (P = 7) now carries exactly the engine's launch bounds. A first attempt that added a
     minimum-blocks bound to it raised its registers to 56 and changed the grid, so it no longer matched the engine.
     Now it has 48 registers and grid 290 on sm_89, and its output is bit-identical.
   - Every other variant carries a minimum of the full kernel's blocks per SM, read from a first build (5 on sm_89).
     No variant spilled.
   - A case where a variant would still need more than one wave is excluded from M2's fits and named in the report.
3. **H9 on sm_75 compares w11b with w13**, both built by the lab's toolchain, W12p absent from w11b. The official
   wheel was built by another toolchain, so its sm_75 count is reported beside it but not used for H9. sm_89 compares
   stock89 with w13sm89, as registered.

Two notes:
- M3 is static. Testing its tool on the laptop builds produced the sm_89 counts the real run will repeat: 1.27 (K=1)
  and 1.26 (K=2) main-loop instructions per weight for w13sm89, against 1.39 and 1.34 for stock89. H8 and H9 were
  registered before these were seen, and are unchanged.
- The laptop driver's smoke run lost its last step because its script was edited while it ran. No data was
  affected. The real runs start from the committed scripts.

## Amendment 2 (2026-11-05 14:27 EST, a guard stop on the laptop)

  changes.
- **Rerun.** The laptop run starts again from round 1, in a new directory. The desktop's real run had not started.
  It runs with the same tools.

## Amendment 3 (2026-11-05 14:36 EST, before the analysis)

On the desktop, M2's full variant reproduced the engine's output in 45 of 48 cases. The three exceptions are grid
shapes that repeat model shapes: 5120×1024 at K = 1, and 5120×10240 at K = 1 and 2.
- **Cause.** The engine logs its W9 choice only the first time it meets a shape. For the repeats the tool found no log
  line and used the kernel's own rule, which is not what the engine ran.
- **Treatment.** By section 2's criterion those cases are not the engine's kernel, so the analysis excludes them and
  names them. Every model shape, all of M2's registered fits, passed.
- **Fix for later runs.** `p9_phases.py` now reuses a shape's logged choice.
- **Laptop.** The laptop runs the kernel's rule, computed in the tool, so it is not affected.

## Phase 1b registration (2026-11-05 15:43 EST, before any 1b measurement): weights held in L2

The cold significance review (`research/raw/2026-11-05-p9-reviews/cold-significance-review.md`) proposed a test that
needs no root: hold the weights in the laptop's 48 MB L2 and compare with the same calls reading DRAM. This separates
waiting on DRAM from waiting inside the SM, which M3 left open and M4 needs root for.

**Measurement (M1b).** Same tool as M1 (`p9_bench.py`), our build on the laptop, the seven model shapes at K = 1 and
2. Each case is timed twice in each of seven rounds, in an order that alternates by round:
- with one copy of its weights (`--copies 1`): every tensor is at most 22.3 MB, so after the first call the replays
  read from L2;
- with the rotated copies as in M1, so the replays read from DRAM.

Warm-up, clocks and the 80 °C pause are as in M1. On the 2080 Ti (L2 5.5 MB), the shapes that fit are timed the
same way as a small check: attn_kv at K = 1 and 2, and gdn_z and gdn_out at K = 1. No fit is made there, only
per-shape ratios.

**Estimands.** On the laptop, b (time per million weights) fitted over the seven shapes for each of L2 and DRAM, at
K = 1 and 2, with t intervals over rounds. The L2 effect is b(L2) / b(DRAM).

**Predictions:**
- **H17** [85%]: positive control. At 2 bits, where the laptop's calls run at the memory's pace, L2 residency lowers
  b by at least 30%: b(L2)/b(DRAM) at most 0.70.
- **H18** [60%]: the 1-bit loop waits inside the SM, not on DRAM. b(L2)/b(DRAM) at K = 1 is at least 0.85.
  - Refuted, so that the loop waits on DRAM: at most 0.70.
  - Unresolved: between 0.70 and 0.85.
- **H19** [55%, descriptive]: on the 2080 Ti, the shapes that fit in L2 run within 10% of their DRAM time at K = 1.

**Analysis.** `p9_analyze.py` gains a section for M1b. Nothing in phase 1's analysis changes.
