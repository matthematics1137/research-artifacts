The premise “measured around October 31” needs refinement. F2 was October 31; the other-engine confirmation was November 4, DFlash2 November 5, same-card quality October 30–November 1, long context November 1–4, and the reported cache-rotated kernel fits November 5. A November 2 patch can be newer than F2 yet already included in P8's competitive baseline.

Each comparison is a slice of the capability landscape: named weight bytes, engine/build, task and sampler, context/cache, single-stream latency and residency. A different model, modified card, concurrent throughput, prefill-inclusive rate or quality benchmark is another coordinate. None can silently replace a point on P8's slice. This survey adds no experimental points to the scoreboard. Future runs must pass the research gate, be registered and queued by the authorized operator, and add their results to the scoreboard.

“Holds” means the dated result remains supported and no material new challenge was located. “At risk” means a present-day interpretation needs the named check; it does not retract the old measurement. “Overtaken” below means only an explicitly identified current public summary was superseded. Searches are bounded evidence, not proof of universal priority. Full queries, retrieval timestamps, versions, exclusions and the PDF checksum are in [searches.md](searches.md).

## Publications and the fixed-call finding

The notable new paper is [FOSCAR, arXiv:2611.09722v1][E14], submitted November 6. Its 2/4-bit **KV-cache** attention kernels target H100/B200. It moves scale/offset work out of per-value dequantization and uses Hopper/Blackwell-specific pipelines; the bottleneck is conversion/instruction issue despite fewer bytes. This strengthens the need to avoid a bytes-only explanation, but does not measure EXL3 weight calls on Turing, fit P8's fixed-per-call term, or implement fd75d's precise target. Archive receipt is in searches.md.

NInferno's new November 11 Turing MMQ launch law is the closest new public engineering overlap: fixed fixup/gap costs plus work proportional to the longest CTA's units, on Q4_K/Q6_K multi-row tiles. Its separate November 11 launch-node constant is Ada-only (1.5 µs; Turing unknown/zero in that model), not the same 8.1 µs measurement. Its fixed-per-speculative-pass hypothesis is dated October 28, outside the requested interval. These deserve credit if P8's explanation expands; they do not support claiming the same 2-bit Turing finding already published [E5].

Older search hits include project-maya issue 23 (opened October 8, two-GPU GLM device/host fixed costs), RK3588 NPU GEMV notes and the archived October 23 Volta kernel bakeoff. They establish that fixed costs/low-bit non-scaling are not generally new ideas, which P8 already respects. None located in this bounded search is an independent post-October-31 replication of the **same** 2-bit EXL3 Turing fit or a replacement for fd75d. This is a “not found” verdict, not proof of absence [E15].
