## Wording the paper can use (the skeptics' versions, kept verbatim in `result.json`)

- **Speed:** "To our knowledge (public web, GitHub, Hugging Face and forks searched through 2026-10-31; Reddit,
  Discord, Zhihu and video could not be read), this is the first published measurement of the unmodified
  Qwen3.8-27B weights running fully on a single stock 11 GB RTX 2080 Ti, and the fastest original-weights decode we
  found on any Turing card with 12 GB or less."
  - Give the counterpoints in the same paragraph. The derivative Bonsai 2 27B on NInfer is faster on this card
    (58.5 / 95.9). Non-Turing cards of 12 GB or less run 2.4-2.5-bit original-weight quants at similar undrafted and
    higher MTP speeds: RTX 3080 10 GB at 45.1 / 64.4; RTX 4070 12 GB with Mirai S at 39.6 / 70.1.
- **Never say:** "first to enable int8 GEMV on Turing", "first original-weights run on 12 GB-or-less Turing", "first
  MTP on 12 GB-or-less Turing" (QWENgine refutes the last two), or "largest Turing speedup".
- **fd75d:** "the first tensor-core decode-attention kernel for exllamav3's 8-bit quantized cache on Turing, to our
  knowledge", with its techniques credited to TurboMind, llama.cpp, fdq4 and kyojin.
- **Efficiency:** "Our absolute speed on the 11 GB card comes largely from a 2.0 bpw quant that fits it ... We
  therefore make no efficiency claim."

## Ideas to borrow, with credit (P8 / P8b backlog)

1. **rafatxf's fdq4 (4-bit-cache tensor-core decode on Turing):** pair it with Paper 6's lesson on KV quantization.
   A Q8 cache cannot reach 64K on 11 GB; a 4-bit cache can. rafatxf reports 28.0 tok/s at 128K on a 22 GB card, and
   8-11x over Triton at 128K. Either adopt fdq4 or extend fd75d to the 4-bit cache, then measure 64K+ quality.
2. **rafatxf's prefill work:** fp16-accumulate GEMM, giving prefill 2.1x / 4.5x / 9.9x at 2K / 16K / 64K. We have
   not touched prefill on the 2080 Ti yet.
3. **buun-llama-cpp's multi-row int8 EXL3 GEMV** (m <= 8 on SM75, batch-independent k-split, M-invariant slice
   heights for determinism). It is the closest public answer to the verify-row problem where our W12c stopped (per-unit
   staging grew with M). Read before any W12d.
4. **NInfer:** int8 IMMA QK attention over a Hadamard-rotated int8 cache on sm_75, at 70-80% of bandwidth on a
   TITAN RTX with original IQ3_S weights. It is the efficiency bar to study.
5. **cookjohn/vllm-turing fdec.cu:** an sm_75 FP8-KV verify/draft attention written for Qwen3.8-27B. Compare its
   tiling with fd75d's.
6. **Mirai S** (a 2.4 bpw QTIP-style trellis quant at 70 tok/s with MTP on an RTX 4070): a different quant format
   worth a later look.
7. **Head-to-head runs the skeptics asked for before any "fastest" wording:**
   - rafatxf's turing branch on our card with our 2.0 bpw model and a Q4 cache. The source is in our mirror and the
     model is on the desktop, so this needs no download.
   - llama.cpp or NInfer with ISTA-DASLab's GSQ-RCO IQ2_XS original-weights GGUF (8.4 GB). That is a model download,
     so it needs Matt's approval.

### group int8
- **C1: int8 GEMV on 64 KB GPUs. Replace exllamav3's blanket 80 KB decline with a per-instance fit check (largest dynamic request plus static shared memory from cudaFuncGetAttributes, against cudaDevAttrMaxSharedMemoryPerBlockOptin, cached per kernel and device), and size the cooperative path's request to its own stage instead of K=8.**: prior_art (high confidence)
  - What remains new: Inside exllamav3 itself, after upstream's 2026-09-26 blanket decline (v1.5.3 to v1.6.5, dev b658e643), no other public patch re-enables the int8 path. The specific implementation also has no exllamav3 precedent: a runtime per-instance fit that declines rather than shrinks (kernel constants unchanged, so host and device decomposition cannot diverge), static memory from cudaFuncGetAttributes, cached per kernel and device, and a coop request sized per K. buun-llama-cpp's 2026-09-09 commit already shows the same cudaFuncGetAttributes cap technique, in a different engine, so the technique itself is not new. Also new: a measured int8-vs-fp16 comparison for EXL3 decode on a stock 11 GB 2080 Ti. I found no published int8-specific Turing speedup from vcruz305, weicj or buun.
  - Safe wording: exllamav3 1.6.5 declines its int8 GEMV on GPUs with less than 80 KB of opt-in shared memory, so the stock wheel never uses it on Turing. Community patches had already run this kernel on 64 KB Turing cards: vcruz305 on a pre-decline base (2026-09-02), and weicj with a fixed 64 KiB budget (2026-09-06).
