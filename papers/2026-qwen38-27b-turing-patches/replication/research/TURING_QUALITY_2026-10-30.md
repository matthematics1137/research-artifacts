## 2. Pre-registered before measurement (written 2026-10-30 12:55 EDT, before either step started)

### 2.1 State precision x drafting (GSM8K-500, steps `q-exl3-mtp-fp32-gsm8k_500`, `q-exl3-nodraft-fp32-gsm8k_500`)

The two fp32-state lanes complete a 2 x 2 with the two stock lanes above. In drafted decoding the GDN recurrent
state is stored once per verification chunk; undrafted, once per token. With fp16 storage the two modes therefore
round the state in different places, which could explain the 10.6% of traces that differ. The matrix products also
run at different batch shapes in verification (M > 1) and plain decode (M = 1), which fp32 storage does not change.

- **H1 (accuracy).** No pair of the four cells differs in accuracy (exact McNemar, two-sided, alpha 0.05).
- **H2 (where the drafted/undrafted divergence comes from).** Byte identity of drafted vs undrafted at fp32 state,
  against 89.4% at fp16:
  - at or above 95%: fp16 state rounding is the main source;
  - within 3 points of 89.4%: the batch-shape arithmetic is the main source;
  - in between: both contribute.
  No direction is predicted; both mechanisms are plausible.
- **H3 (replication at matched state precision).** TURING-exl3-mtp-fp32 vs the laptop's E47 MTP (fp32 state on
  1.4.7): accuracy not different (McNemar). Byte identity is reported against 87.2% at fp16, with the laptop's own
  run-to-run bound stated beside it.

### 2.2 Decode kernel census (step `census-w2`; `tools/turing/census.sh`, `census_report.py`)

nsys captures only the decode of three prompts (code, math, prose; 256 new tokens each), undrafted and MTP, at
the lane settings (16K Q8 cache, chunk 1024, MTP 6 dynamic), after a warm-up on the same prompts.

- **E1.** Undrafted decode is GPU-bound: the GPU is busy for at least 85% of each token's wall time. If idle
  time is over 15%, host overhead (launches, Python, synchronization) is a lever before any kernel work.
- **E2.** The EXL3 trellis matrix kernels (`exl3_gemv*`, `exl3_gemm*`, `exl3_mgemm*`) take more than 60% of
  undrafted kernel time; the GDN recurrent and attention kernels together take less than 20%.
- **E3.** The census names the plan's next step. If the int8 GEMV family never appears, the int8 fit gate (W7)
  applies to every quantized matrix. The family with the largest time per token whose achieved bandwidth is
  furthest below the card's 616 GB/s gets the first kernel work.

## 3. Decode census results (2026-10-30 13:04-13:09 EDT; `testsuite/results/turing/census/w2`)

| config | ms per token under nsys (code / math / prose) | GPU busy | kernels per token |
|---|---|---|---|
| undrafted | 45.2 / 44.2 / 47.4 | 96.0-96.7% | 869 |
| MTP (6, dynamic) | 21.4 / 20.4 / 37.0 (acceptance 0.66 / 0.70 / 0.32) | 88.3-90.8% | 287-570 |

nsys adds about 5% (undrafted 22.3 tok/s captured against 23.6 tok/s served).

- **E1 holds.** Undrafted decode keeps the GPU busy 96% of each token. Host overhead (launches, Python, sync) is
  about 1.5-1.8 ms per token and is not the lever. Under MTP the idle share rises to 9-12% (2.4-3.4 ms per token),
  in the draft and verify bookkeeping.
- **E2 holds.** The EXL3 matrix kernels take 92.0% of undrafted kernel time: `exl3_gemv` 53.2%, `exl3_mgemm` 23.0%,
  `exl3_gemm` 15.8%. Attention (split and combine), the GDN recurrence, conv and norms together take about 7%.
- **E3.** The int8 GEMV family never appears (W7 applies to every mul1 matrix of this model). Per-layer bitrates
  explain the rest of the dispatch: `research/TURING_W7_INT8_GEMV_2026-10-30.md`, W6 section.
  - The worst per-byte item is the K = 1 matrices on the tensor-core GEMM, at about 74 GB/s.
  - The largest slow item is the fused GDN qkv + z MGEMM: 21% of kernel time at about 123 GB/s.
  - The narrow-config GEMV (down_proj, out_proj, o_proj) takes 26% at about 160-180 GB/s.
  - So the first work is dispatch switches (W6 arms G1-G4), before any kernel is written.
- **Bandwidth.** Undrafted decode reads about 6.6 GB of weights per token (`census/weight_bytes_*.txt`). Over the
  matrix kernels' 40.3 ms per token that is about 164 GB/s, 27% of 616 GB/s.
