\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Self-drafting can accelerate a memory-constrained language model while
consuming memory needed for context. We measure that tradeoff for
Qwen3.8-27B on a 12\,GB RTX~4080 Laptop GPU capped at 80\,W, using a
2.00-bit EXL3 quantization and low-bit GGUF comparators. With its own
multi-token-prediction head, EXL3 completion throughput rises from about 29 to
48.7--50.4 tokens/s on code and math and 35.7 on prose. Sustained drafted
runs deliver 0.559 tokens/J for EXL3 and 0.577 for GGUF, measured from
GPU-board power. Reducing the server's concurrency allocation from four
slots to one lowers drafted load memory from 10,226 to 8,050\,MiB.
Drafted accuracy is 97.6 versus 97.2\% on 500 paired GSM8K items and 86.8
versus 86.2\% on 500 paired MMLU items at temperature zero; these observations
show no measured decrease but do not establish equivalence. Source-level
diagnostics explain the recurrent-state allocation and identify prefill and
draft acceptance bottlenecks in the tested engines. Exposing an existing
checkpoint interval also reduces one edited-prompt latency from 10.1 to
5.0\,s, at a cost in host memory and cold prefill. This is a deployment
benchmark with configuration changes, not a new decoding method. Results
describe one loaded laptop and pinned software; most EXL3 measurements used
a mixed CUDA library environment, and performance on newer releases is untested.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

On a 12\,GB GPU, the memory used to accelerate a quantized model competes
with its context window. Qwen3.8-27B makes this tradeoff concrete: its native
multi-token-prediction (MTP) head proposes tokens for the main model to verify,
but speculative rollback also requires stored recurrent states. The useful
deployment question is whether the speed and energy gains justify that
memory, and which serving settings change the tradeoff.

Prior work already establishes speculative decoding and its energy benefits
\citep{leviathan2023fast,chen2023accelerating,dutta2026specenergy}. Author
benchmarks also measure Qwen3.8-27B with MTP on desktop RTX~3090 cards,
including EXL3 and GGUF deployments \citep{ollman2026qwen38,ang2026qwen38,
tsai2026rtx3090spec}. Their different precision, power, prompts and software
do not determine the result on this 80\,W laptop. Our question is narrower:
what speed, energy, memory and tested accuracy does self-drafting deliver
for the low-bit artifacts that fit this machine?

For the 2.00-bpw EXL3 artifact, MTP increases completion throughput from about 29 to
48.7--50.4 tokens/s on code and math and 35.7 on prose. Sustained EXL3 and
GGUF runs measure 0.559 and 0.577 tokens/J; without replicated sustained
runs, that small difference is not an established ranking. Allocating one
concurrent job rather than the server's four-slot default saves
2,176\,MiB on the drafted EXL3 load. Paired tests on 1,000 greedy GSM8K and
MMLU items show no measured accuracy decrease, while exposing a separate
run-to-run reproducibility problem in the tested exllamav3~1.4.7 build.

The contribution is a hardware-specific benchmark and a set of tested
configuration changes, backed by archived measurements and source-level
diagnostics. A checkpoint-interval experiment led to a merged TabbyAPI
configuration interface; a nondeterminism report led to a maintainer fix
checked here on 100 items. We do not propose MTP, recurrent checkpointing,
or a new quantizer, and do not claim that the tested settings remain optimal
as engines change. Results are organized by deployment question; the
appendix retains the experiment identifiers, detailed tables and failed
predictions needed to audit the evidence.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Hardware and operating conditions.}
One laptop: an NVIDIA RTX~4080 Laptop GPU with 12,282\,MiB of device memory
(E12) under an 80\,W power limit, an Intel Core i9-14900HX, and 62\,GiB of
RAM. The desktop session holds about 485\,MiB of VRAM (E23), leaving about
11.5\,GiB, of which the campaign kept roughly 10.5\,GiB as its working
envelope for a model process (E12). The machine was not idle: foreign agents ran
throughout and one foreign Python job held about four CPU cores, so every
row is ``loaded machine'' unless stated. GPU power, clocks, and temperature
were sampled with \q{nvidia-smi} at 1--2\,s; energy is GPU-board energy only,
with no wall meter, and idle draw was measured for 30\,s with the model
loaded before each sustained run (E25).

\paragraph{Model.}
Qwen3.8-27B is a dense hybrid of the Qwen3.5 family \citep{qwen2026qwen38card}:
64 layers of which 48 are Gated-DeltaNet (GDN) linear-attention layers
\citep{yang2024gated} and 16 full attention, a 248,320-token vocabulary, and a
one-layer MTP head that can draft for its own decoder. Each GDN layer holds a
recurrent state of 48 value heads $\times$ 128 $\times$ 128 in fp32, 3\,MiB
per layer and about 148\,MiB per sequence (E28); the attention layers' Q8 KV
cache costs 8.5\,MiB per 256-token page. Both engines keep the recurrent
state in fp32 (source check, H16; cf.\ \citealp{zhang2026damp}). The model
runs in thinking mode with the vendor's recommended sampling (temperature
1.0, top-$p$ 0.95, top-$k$ 20) and \q{reasoning\_effort: medium} unless
stated.

\paragraph{Quantized artifacts.}
Table~\ref{tab:artifacts} lists the files. All eleven local Qwen3.8-27B
artifacts were hashed and matched to the Hub's LFS SHA-256 records on
2026-09-06 (\q{hash\_audit\_2026-09-06.json}); the Unsloth repository was
re-cut on 2026-08-20, so the hashes rather than the names identify the
evidence. The EXL3 file is turboderp's \q{SC\_2.00bpw\_H3} branch
\citep{turboderp2026qwen38exl3}: a trellis-coded quantization in the QTIP
family \citep{tseng2024qtip} with 14 tensors at 1~bit, 334 at 2, 38 at 3,
and 14 at 4, a 3-bit output head, and a 2-bit MTP layer, calibrated on 217
reasoning traces (613K tokens) generated by the model's own 6-bit
quantization (E32). The GGUF files are Unsloth's dynamic quantizations
\citep{unsloth2026qwen38gguf}; their MTP head ships as a separate 1.28\,GiB
sidecar, about 80\% of it duplicated target tensors that a lab patch lets a
0.25\,GiB stripped sidecar borrow instead (E12). The DFlash2 block drafter on
the \q{UD-IQ2\_XXS} lane is z-lab's \q{Q4\_K\_M} GGUF of the DFlash
architecture \citep{chen2026dflash,zlab2026dflash2gguf}.

\paragraph{Engines and patches.}
The EXL3 lane is TabbyAPI \citep{theroyallab2025tabbyapi} on
exllamav3~1.4.7 \citep{turboderp2025exllamav3}, staged in isolation from the
lab's pinned 1.4.3 (undrafted decode identical within $-0.2$ to $+1.5\%$ on
four prompts, E7; 1.4.7 fixes a slot leak in the draft path), with a Q8
cache, \q{chunk\_size} 1,024 (2,048 in the early 16K runs), and
\q{draft\_mode: mtp} with dynamic draft sizing. The GGUF lane is llama.cpp
v0.4.0 \citep{gerganov2023llamacpp} (commit \q{5266f24}, CUDA 12.6, sm\_89)
with the pinned 2026-08-26 build (\q{035e227}) as a control; the builds agree
within 1\% on undrafted decode and short prefill (E1/E2, three \q{llama-bench}
repeats). GGUF runs used \q{-ngl 99 -np 1 -fa on -ctk q8\_0 -ctv q8\_0 -ctxcp
4 -cms 1024 -b 512 -ub 256} (E4a). Four lab patches, off unless their
environment variable is set, instrument or extend the staged engines
(Section~\ref{sec:repro}).\footnote{A fifth, a CPU fallback for a CUDA
Cholesky failure met while converting the E32, E41, and E42 quants on this
box, was withdrawn on 2026-09-10 as a non-bug once the failure was traced to
the library-path fault described next (E50); it only widened the trigger of
the converter's existing CPU fallback, and its upstream pull request was
closed (Section~\ref{sec:repro}).} The single-user EXL3 configuration the
results converge on---SC\_2.00 with MTP dynamic drafting on one slot---is
called the daily driver below; its final form is specified in
Section~\ref{sec:repro}, and rows that predate its last revision state their
own configuration.

\paragraph{Library environment (E50).}
Every EXL3-lane measurement here (E7--E49, and the quant conversions of E32,
E41, and E42) ran under a mixed CUDA library set: the lab's shell exported
\q{LD\_LIBRARY\_PATH=/usr/local/cuda-12.1/lib64}, so torch~2.10.0+cu128
loaded the system cuBLASLt and NVRTC 12.1 beside its bundled 12.8 libraries
(read from \q{/proc/<pid>/maps}); the fault was found on 2026-09-10, after
the campaign. Two pre-registered controls on 100 GSM8K items under a fully
bundled set (H38d, E50; loaded libraries recorded per run) left the E49
non-reproducibility in place (64/100 undrafted same-config identity clean
against 50/100 mixed) and the drafted wall time inside run-to-run variance
(5.92\,s per item clean against 6.39 and 5.87\,s mixed), so the throughput
rows stand as measured under the mixed set (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

\paragraph{Measurements, suites, and statistics.}
Timing boundaries differ by engine and must not be interpreted as matched
kernel-only decode rates. Throughput is the server's own \q{predicted\_per\_second} on llama.cpp and
completion tokens over end-to-end wall time on TabbyAPI, which returns no
timings (tokens counted through \q{/v1/token/encode}, E7). The EXL3 rate
therefore includes prefill and request overhead; within-lane comparisons
retain that same boundary. Timed generations
use four prompt types (a math word problem, a code function with tests, a
400-word essay, a whole-file rewrite) at a 1,024-token cap, effort medium,
temperature 1.0, with two repeats per prompt (E4, E7, E9) or three (E36,
E44); resident VRAM is the per-process figure after load and after one warm
request (E3, E27). Quality suites are the lab's HumanEval+ 20
\citep{chen2021humaneval,liu2023evalplus}, a 25-item MATH subset (\q{math25},
raw and lenient rescoring) \citep{hendrycks2021math}, GSM8K-100
\citep{cobbe2021gsm8k} (some historical rows are a 50-item sample), MMLU-200
\citep{hendrycks2021mmlu}, and a six-item needle suite (8K, 16K, 32K prompts,
two positions), at a 4,096-token completion cap, effort medium, temperature
1.0, one run per item; a cap-truncated item without an answer scores wrong.
Paired comparisons use the discordant items and the exact two-sided McNemar
test \citep{mcnemar1947}. One same-configuration repeat of GSM8K-100 moved
from 98 to 95 correct (3 lost, 0 gained, $p=0.25$; E26). This illustrates
sampling variation; it does not estimate a general three-item noise band.
The larger temperature-zero comparisons use 1,000 paired items for drafting
and 200 for the higher-bit reference (Section~\ref{sec:hardening}). We report
observed differences and paired tests, not statistical equivalence.

\paragraph{Pre-registration and evidence boundary.}
Predictions and falsifiers were recorded before each experiment block,
starting on 2026-09-06 and ending with the release control on 2026-09-19.
The artifact's design record preserves the dated amendments, evidence commits,
and subsequent corrections. Experiment labels E1--E52 identify those frozen
records, rather than a proposed order of reading. The claim ledger distinguishes
values recomputed from archived rows from values documented only in logs or
the experiment record. Later literature and software changes inform the
discussion but do not alter the frozen measurements.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Throughput and tokens per joule}
\label{sec:throughput}

Drafting the EXL3 quant with its own MTP head raises completion throughput from 29 to
48.7--50.4 tok/s on code and math (1.67--1.74$\times$) and to 35.7 on prose
(1.2$\times$), missing the pre-registered $\ge1.8\times$ and $\ge1.4\times$
(H1) by a small and a large margin (E7, four prompt types, means over two
sessions; Table~\ref{tab:throughput} gives every timed configuration). The
same head as a sidecar gives 1.8$\times$ on the GGUF lane (E4b), and the
external DFlash2 block drafter gives 2.0--2.6$\times$ on structured content
but 1.2$\times$ on prose, where MTP is 15--18\% faster (E4a; H2's prose
clauses falsified). Because the card sits at the cap in every row
(79.2--79.8\,W, E4a), tokens per joule are approximately throughput divided
by 80\,W; joules per token are power divided by throughput:
drafting halves joules per token on structured content and saves 15--27\% on
prose (E4a). Decode is insensitive to the engine version ($<1\%$ between
llama.cpp builds, E1/E2; $-0.2$ to $+1.5\%$ between exllamav3 versions, E7)
and to temperature (0.7 versus 1.0 moves acceptance by at most five points
and throughput by 1--7\%; E9); with depth it loses 15.2\% from 2K to 24K on
the GGUF lane (E20) and 22\% from 2K to 30K on the EXL3 lane at 36K context,
past H28's 20\% falsifier (E38).

The sustained runs (Table~\ref{tab:energy}) deliver 0.559 and 0.577 tokens
per joule gross (0.704 and 0.740 net of idle). The 3.2\% difference is
descriptive: one run per lane at unequal durations does not establish an
energy ranking. Median SM clocks are 1,215\,MHz on EXL3 and 1,380\,MHz on
llama.cpp;
the cap was active in 96--98\% of samples at 52--64\,$^\circ$C with no
thermal-slowdown reason once generation started, the only drift a cold-start
step inside minutes 0--5 on the EXL3 lane (46.6 to 44.2 tok/s; E25, H9
confirmed). Prose is the least efficient output per joule (0.50) because it
drafts worst. Continuous batching turns the fixed cap into a multiplier
(E29): two drafted streams reach 1.59$\times$ the aggregate and 0.86 tok/J,
four reach 2.12$\times$ and 1.15 tok/J, and drafting keeps the lead at every
batch size (91.0 versus 75.6 tok/s at four), although undrafted batching
scales more steeply from a lower base; the per-stream loss at four drafted
streams ($-46\%$) exceeds H20's 40\% falsifier, as expected where a shared
dynamic draft window makes every row pay verification for the longest window
(engine reading; \citealp{su2023synergy}).

\subsection{Draft acceptance, draft length, and memory allocation}
\label{sec:drafting}

\paragraph{Limited benefit from the tested verifier change.}
Acceptance is set by content---0.54 on prose, 0.70 on code, 0.73 on math,
and 0.74 on rewrites over 9,821 drafted positions on the daily driver,
decaying with draft position (0.77, 0.66, 0.53, 0.37 for positions 0--3;
E35)---and short tool-call replies in an agent loop draft worse than long
math (0.28--0.70 on llama.cpp, 0.50 on TabbyAPI), so decode there sits at
29--43 tok/s rather than 45--50 (E18, ten turns). Both engines draft the
argmax of the MTP head and verify by sampling the target through the job's
sampler and accepting on equality, so per-position acceptance is $p(x^*)$,
the target's filtered probability of the drafted token; standard speculative
sampling with a sampled draft $x\sim q$
\citep{leviathan2023fast,chen2023accelerating} accepts with probability
$\sum_v \min(p(v),q(v))$ at the same output distribution, and an MTP-specific
analysis attributes entropy-bounded acceptance to exactly this rule
\citep{li2026bebop}. H32 predicted that the sampled rule would gain
$\ge0.10$ on prose. Table~\ref{tab:specmeas} records the measurement-only
patch's per-set quantities: $p(x^*)$ predicts the observed acceptance within 0.007 on
every set, but $\sum\min(p,q)$ sits within $\pm0.035$ of it everywhere,
$+0.018$ on prose, because the MTP head is more confident than the target on
its own argmax ($q(x^*)$ 0.69--0.83 against $p(x^*)$ 0.54--0.74) and puts the
rest of its mass on tokens the target does not favor. H32 is falsified. The
sampled-draft verifier was nevertheless implemented and A/B-tested (H33,
E36): its small correctness checks passed (temperature-0 outputs byte-identical on three
prompts---a check that predates the E49 finding of
Section~\ref{sec:hardening}---and 300-completion histograms at temperature~1
consistent with the stock verifier) and reaches its
theoretical acceptance (0.592 on 19,074 positions against
$\sum\min(p,q)=0.597$), but end-to-end speed fell on code ($-5\%$), math
($-8\%$), and rewrite ($-3\%$; medians of three repeats) and was unchanged on
prose (34.3 to 33.9 tok/s). This verifier change did not improve throughput
with the stock head. A different head or verification implementation remains
an untested possibility, not a consequence ruled out by this experiment.

\paragraph{Draft length under dynamic drafting (H37, E44).}
The static cap \q{draft\_num\_tokens} still matters under dynamic drafting:
across 2, 3, 4, and 6 the spread is 6\% on prose, 7\% on code, 8\% on math,
and 11\% on rewrite (medians of three repeats; Table~\ref{tab:draftlen}),
but the pre-registered shape was wrong. Two is slowest on code, math and rewrite, while four is slowest on prose.
Six is fastest on prose and math; three narrowly leads code (46.9 versus
46.8 tokens/s), and four leads rewrite. Six costs 256\,MiB more VRAM than
four. Dynamic sizing therefore does not eliminate the tradeoff between the
configured cap, workload and memory.

\paragraph{The recurrent-state history tax and \q{max\_batch\_size} (H19, E27/E28).}
Enabling the drafter cost a fixed 2,432\,MiB of process VRAM that moved
neither with context nor with the draft cache's precision
(Table~\ref{tab:vram}). Instrumented loads and a source reading agree on where
it goes: for speculative rollback the main cache pre-allocates, per GDN
layer, an fp32 tensor of shape (\q{max\_batch\_size}, \q{max\_history}$+1$,
48, 128, 128)---3\,MiB per slot and history step across 48 layers---and
TabbyAPI sets \q{max\_history} to the draft length (4) whenever a draft model
exists and defaults \q{max\_batch\_size} to 4 for recurrent models:
$4\times5$ states $=2{,}880$\,MiB drafted against 576\,MiB undrafted, which
with the MTP weights and draft cache predicts 2,437--2,453\,MiB against 2,432
measured (E28). Each drafted slot costs 728\,MiB at draft length 4 (438 at 2,
148 undrafted). For a single user, \q{max\_batch\_size: 1} reclaims
2,176\,MiB (10,226 to 8,050\,MiB at load) with decode within noise and
byte-identical temperature-0 outputs on three 120-token prompts, and lets
the drafted server load 32K context at 8,754\,MiB and survive a 30K-token
prompt (E28; H19 confirmed on every clause). The allocation follows the tested
engine's snapshot scheme; other rollback designs can change that cost
(Section~\ref{sec:related}).

\subsection{Paired accuracy and output reproducibility}
\label{sec:hardening}

The small temperature-1 drafted-versus-undrafted checks (E15, E17, E21;
Table~\ref{tab:t1quality}) did not resolve an accuracy decrease. A larger
paired block therefore ran at temperature~0 on $n=500$ supersets of the suites
with each output hashed (8.0\,h of GPU for H38a, 1.7\,h for H38b, 52\,min
for H38c, plus the three 100-item runs of the E50 library-set control).
Table~\ref{tab:h38} summarizes it and Tables~\ref{tab:h38a}--\ref{tab:h38c}
give the full records; the predictions and falsifiers are restated in
Appendix~\ref{app:tables} (H38a: $\ge95\%$ byte-identical text on 1,000
paired items with accuracy within 1.0 point at exact McNemar $p\ge0.05$;
H38b: within 2.0 points of \q{UD-Q4\_K\_XL}, llama.cpp with 30 of 64 layers
on the GPU, on 200 GSM8K items at mean length within 15\%). These historical
decision rules are recorded faithfully, but a non-significant test plus a
small point estimate is not an equivalence test; we do not interpret them as such.

E47 supported the accuracy clause on both suites, 97.6 against 97.2\% on
GSM8K-500 (0/2 discordant, $p=0.50$) and 86.8 against 86.2\% on MMLU-500
(6/9, $p=0.61$), the drafted side nominally ahead with 11 of the 17
discordant items (pooled $p=0.33$), and the temperature-1 speed-ups
reproduced under greedy decoding (1.79$\times$ and 1.48$\times$). The
identity clause was falsified, and not narrowly: 63.6\% and 21.0\%
byte-identical against the 90\% falsifier. The differing pairs almost all
agree on the answer (179 of 182 on GSM8K, 374 of the 387 parsed on MMLU),
and identity falls with trace length as a constant per-token hazard,
$P(\text{identical})=(1-\varepsilon)^L$ with $\varepsilon\approx1.6\times10^{-3}$
and $2.6\times10^{-3}$: one greedy argmax flip every 400--600 tokens
(Table~\ref{tab:h38a}). E48
supported H38b on both clauses: 99.0\% for the 2-bit quant against 98.0\% for
the 4-bit GGUF (1/3 discordant, $p=0.63$) at mean completion tokens of 322.0
against 321.9, so the $+26\%$ inflation of Section~\ref{sec:inflation} does
not appear in this GSM8K comparison under greedy decoding. The contrast
suggests task- and sampling-dependent length effects; it does not identify
their cause. The marker penalty was not tested on this larger paired block.
Recorded, not an intrinsic speed comparison: 6.30\,s per item
for the resident 2-bit quant against 30.24\,s for the offloaded 4-bit one.

Whether the hazard belonged to the drafter's batched verify forward or to
the engine was H38c (Table~\ref{tab:h38c}). A same-config re-run of the
drafted daily driver on 100 GSM8K items, pre-registered at $\ge97\%$
identity on the assumption that the engine is deterministic at batch~1,
came out at 91/100, between the prediction and the $\le90\%$ falsifier; 25
paired greedy traces with top-2 log-probabilities per token put all five
divergences at near-ties (undrafted top-1/top-2 gap at most 0.031 nat, the
drafted token the runner-up in five of five), consistent with numerical
near-ties rather than evidence specific to a verifier defect. Two undrafted
runs of the same config, predicted at
$\ge97\%$ identity in a control added before it ran, agreed on 50/100, past
the $\le90\%$ falsifier. The engine's own single-token greedy decode is
thus not bit-reproducible on this model under exllamav3~1.4.7, with a
per-token hazard of $2.5\times10^{-3}$, about seven times the drafted
path's $3.4\times10^{-4}$; one flip changes the rest of the trace and
almost never the answer (50 of 50 differing pairs). Because those runs used
the mixed library set of Section~\ref{sec:setup}, H38d re-ran the undrafted
control under a fully bundled set, pre-registered on 2026-09-10 before it
ran at $\ge97\%$ identity if the non-determinism were a library artifact
($\le60\%$ the falsifier): it measured 64/100 [54.2, 72.7] (E50), inside
the interval of the mixed 50/100, clean run 1 agreeing with the two mixed
runs on 58 and 61 items, and drafted clean against drafted mixed at 91/100,
the same as mixed against mixed. Thus engine-level variation confounds the
drafted-versus-undrafted identity comparison under either library set. The
supported accuracy statement is that drafting showed no measured decrease
on these 1,000 items. The higher-bit comparison likewise records 99.0 versus
98.0\% and nearly equal mean length on 200 GSM8K items, without proving
equivalence. Run-to-run numerical nondeterminism is known in LLM inference
\citep{yuan2025nondeterminism}; these controls show why an identity test on
the measured build needs an undrafted same-configuration reference.

Following an upstream report on 2026-09-10, the maintainer added a deterministic reduction in the
Gated-DeltaNet kernel on exllamav3's development branch (commit 900cf17,
built and tested here at the branch head 08849e3, after the 1.4.9 release). Built here against the pinned torch and run under
the same clean library set, two undrafted greedy runs on the same 100 items
were byte-identical on 100 of 100 with accuracy 97 and 97 (E51; predicted at
$\ge97\%$ before the run); against 1.4.7 the same prompts agree on 67/100 at
equal accuracy, the expected signature of a changed reduction order. The fix
shipped in the released exllamav3 1.5.0: its official wheel, served
unmodified through the same pinned environment, again gave two byte-identical
greedy runs on 100 of 100 items, accuracy 98 and 98 (E52; pre-registered as
H38f). The non-identity of E47, E49 and E50 is therefore a property of the
measured 1.4.7 build, and the identity clause of H38a becomes a meaningful
test on the tested 1.5.0 configuration. The 100-item repeat does not establish
determinism for every input, batch shape or later release.

\subsection{Prefill anatomy and the checkpoint interval}
\label{sec:prefill}

Prefill is slower on the measured EXL3 path and can dominate an edited-prompt
request. Profiled on the bare API for
one 3,652-token prefill after warm-up (E37; 1,515\,MHz at 79.8\,W), GPU time
splits 73.8\% cuBLAS fp16 GEMM, 10.7\% trellis-to-fp16 reconstruction, 4.8\%
copies, 3.5\% Gated-DeltaNet chunk kernels, 2.9\% paged attention, and about
4\% norms and miscellany, at 583 tok/s with a 1,024-token chunk (535 at 512,
592 at 2,048). Every chunk rebuilds the whole 2-bit body as fp16 (about
0.24\,s per 1,024-token chunk, no cache mode) and multiplies it with fp32
accumulation at 37 TFLOPS against a 43.7 TFLOPS FLOP-weighted ceiling for
these shapes. Doubling the default chunk improves this measurement by only
1.5\%, although 512 to 2,048 improves it by 10.7\%. The profile identifies
where time is spent; it does not rule out other kernels, caching strategies
or allocation changes. The GGUF lane uses a different integer tile-dequant
path and prefills at 600--710 tok/s
(E20) and holds 686--690 tok/s at 8K and 609--618 at 32K with matched KV
types (E5), making it faster on these measured prefills. On the
GGUF lane the prefill collapse the lab had recorded at 32K is a configuration
artifact: the mismatched pair \q{-ctk q8\_0 -ctv q4\_0} runs attention off the
fused kernels at 13--15\% GPU utilization, 57 tok/s at 8K and past a 600-s
deadline at 32K (E5, one needle item per cell; H4 confirmed), and the needle
suite goes from 4/6 to 6/6 with 32K items answered in 49\,s (E16a).

Two engine behaviors set the latency an agent sees. A repeated prefix is
cheap on both lanes because the recurrent state is checkpointed: append-only
turns of a synthetic twelve-turn agent conversation cost a flat 1.8--2.0\,s of
prefill per roughly 1.1K new tokens on llama.cpp with default checkpoint
settings (E6; H5's failure prediction did not materialize) and 2.0--2.6\,s on
TabbyAPI (E18b2), and a cold 4K system prefix that took 9.3--9.4\,s is served
in 0.9\,s on its second use and still after an unrelated conversation, from a
148\,MiB host-RAM checkpoint---with room for one checkpoint the reuse is lost
(E30, two repeats; H21 confirmed). An edited prompt, by contrast, replays from
the last checkpoint at or before the edit, never from the edit: an edited
header at turn six costs one full re-prefill (13.0--13.1\,s at about 7.8K
tokens) on both engines (E18), and because TabbyAPI's ingestion checkpoints
are sparse by default, any edit before token 4,096 of a 5,237-token document
costs the full 9.9\,s and only an edit after it costs 3.0\,s (E38; H29's
mechanism confirmed, its predicted positions falsified).
Table~\ref{tab:editcost} shows the lab patch that exposes the interval: at
1,024 tokens an edit 2,200 tokens from the end costs 5.0\,s instead of
10.1\,s and the cost becomes proportional to the distance from the edit to
the end of the prompt, for $+2$--$3\%$ on the cold prefill and 148\,MiB of
host RAM per extra checkpoint (E43; H35 confirmed). The pinned TabbyAPI
checkout does not expose the knob; the patch is two lines of engine code and
one passthrough, and the passthrough was merged into TabbyAPI after these
measurements (pull request \#468, 2026-09-21).

\subsection{Two-bit reasoning length: the inflation, its mechanism, and the levers}
\label{sec:inflation}

The tested lower-bit artifacts produce longer outputs on math25: on matched items the median completion is 861 tokens at
\q{UD-Q4\_K\_XL}, 1,083 for the drafted EXL3 2.00 ($+26\%$), 1,381--1,447 for
\q{UD-IQ2\_S} ($+60$--$68\%$), and 1,466 for \q{UD-IQ2\_XXS} ($+70\%$) (E8,
$n=25$, temperature~1; H14 confirmed), with no inflation on GSM8K (409--434
mean tokens across tiers, $n=50$; R0). Table~\ref{tab:traces} examines the output differences using temperature-0 traces and per-token log-probabilities on the
same 25 math and 20 code prompts (E40). The extra length is entirely in the
math reasoning and it is re-verification: 2.4$\times$ the ``Wait''-class
transitions per trace and explicit self-corrections up from 1 to 4, while
code does not inflate (0.98$\times$). The second half of the pre-registered
mechanism came out the opposite of what was predicted: the 2-bit model is
not less sure at those points---its greedy tokens fall below $p=0.5$ less
often (1.4\% versus 2.3\%), the transition tokens sit at a similar median
probability (0.84 versus 0.80), and the traces already differ at the third
token (median two matching tokens on 24 of 25 math items)---so nothing in
per-token confidence marks the branch; final answers agree on 22 of 25 items.
H26's transition clause held and its low-confidence, divergence, and
corrections clauses were falsified. The reading that survives---the
quantization shifts the model's policy toward more checking on hard math, a
confident behavioral drift rather than noise at high-entropy branch
points---is an inference from these counts, not a measured cause.

\paragraph{The levers.}
Table~\ref{tab:levers} collects every intervention the campaign tried against
the tax, each against its pre-registered prediction. Three cheap levers on
the quantization itself are nulls. Recalibrating the stock per-tensor recipe
on 155 task-focused reasoning traces the 2-bit model wrote itself (166K
tokens, filled to the converter's 250 rows with turboderp's own trace) moved
completion tokens within $\pm6\%$, with no statistically resolved accuracy
change on the three small suites (E32; H23 falsified). The stock quant
already uses self-generated reasoning calibration
\citep{williams2024selfcal,wang2026ayot}. Moving bits at a constant
2.00\,bpw from the lowest-error 3-bit and 4-bit projections to the six worst
1-bit MLP tensors cut those tensors' proxy error fivefold, yet by layer 50
the two error measures were similar (0.08490 versus 0.08457), without the
predicted downstream token reduction (E41; H25 falsified): the output-side error re-equilibrates
within a dozen layers, in line with the ``damage is diffuse'' finding for
other models \citep{hu2026damage}. The sampled-draft verifier is the third
null (E36). The control that turns the calibration null into a result is
off-policy calibration (H31, E42): the same recipe converted on 250 rows of
generic prose and standard-library Python raised GSM8K-100 completion tokens
by 15.6\% and math25 by 6.3\%, quintupled math25 cap truncations (1 to 5),
and lost items on every suite; no single suite reaches significance, but
the discordant items run 9 to 1 for the self-calibrated quant across the
three suites (exact binomial $p=0.021$). The off-policy recipe worsened these measurements; the tested on-policy
recipe did not yield the predicted improvement. This does not exhaust
possible calibration choices.

Of the decoding-time levers, the overthinking-marker logit penalty
\citep{lotfi2026longer} is a lever on the GGUF lanes (math25 median token
ratios at $-2.0$: 0.85 on \q{UD-IQ2\_XXS}, 0.94 on \q{UD-IQ2\_S}, 1.03 on EXL3;
E13, E19) and a small observed saving on the trellis lane, where a strength sweep
(E45) found the accuracy clause holding at every strength (0--2 items, all
$p\ge0.5$) but the saving flat at about 5\% from $-0.5$ to $-3.0$ and largest
at the weakest setting (H36's monotonicity clause falsified): half a nat is
enough to route around the marker token, and the model then re-verifies in
other words. A client-side trial-answer early exit \citep{yang2025deer} saved
18--24\% of GSM8K-100 tokens and 6--20\% of math25 tokens at an accuracy cost
equal in size to the change in one baseline repeat, but not thereby
established as harmless (E24, E26); forcing an
answer at 90\% of the budget produced 0 correct answers on the 4 items it
fired on (E31; H22 falsified), consistent with the report that an injected
\q{</think>} does not end reasoning \citep{koh2026think}; lowering
\q{reasoning\_effort} to low collapsed HumanEval+ while taking longer on it
(E14; H10 falsified in the opposite direction); and the 2.20-bpw branch buys
nothing measurable at 608\,MiB more VRAM (E23c; H18 confirmed).

\subsection{Shared serving for two or three agents}
\label{sec:shared}

A box that runs several agents can serve them from one drafted TabbyAPI, but
the recurrent-history slots price the choice (Table~\ref{tab:agents}; E39:
synthetic agents with 3.7K-token system prompts and six append-only
700-token turns, about 8.2K tokens by the last). The pre-registered
three-slot, 16K configuration fails: three multi-turn agents need about 24K
tokens of KV by their last turns, so a 16K cache evicts and re-prefills
continuously, later-turn time to first token climbs to 27.6\,s, and the
process reaches 10.91\,GiB (H27 falsified). With two slots and a 24K cache
two agents each keep 34.3 tok/s ($-33\%$ versus 51.3 alone) at 3.1\,s
later-turn TTFT, but the aggregate gain is 1.31$\times$, under the
1.59$\times$ of continuous batching (E29), because each new turn's prefill
stalls the other agent's decode and the agents idle between turns; a third
agent queues and pushes later-turn TTFT to 13.7\,s at no aggregate gain. A
two-slot drafted server at 24K is the better of these tested shared
configurations (\q{serving/tabby-2agents.yml}). More clients queue in this
configuration. An undrafted server uses 148 rather than 728\,MiB per slot,
but its single-stream rate is lower (28.9 versus 43.0 tok/s, E29); its
suitability for a particular multi-agent workload requires a separate test.

\section{Negative Results}
\label{sec:negative}

The detailed prediction ledger is in Table~\ref{tab:negative}. Several
plausible changes did not deliver their predicted improvement: the
sampled-draft verifier was no faster; one on-policy calibration and one
constant-bit reallocation did not substantially shorten outputs; forced
early answers failed on all four affected math items; and four-way batching
raised aggregate throughput while reducing each stream's speed by 46\%.
These are bounded negative results for named implementations and samples,
not evidence that the broader approaches cannot work. The small accuracy
comparisons remain inconclusive about modest gains or harms.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

For a single user, the clearest configuration gain is reducing unused
concurrency allocation. The recurrent-history tensor's shape predicts the
observed drafting overhead, and changing the slot count lowers load memory
by 2,176\,MiB. This preserves space for context without changing the weights.
For repeated requests that edit cached text, denser recurrent checkpoints
offer a separate latency--host-memory tradeoff. These are actionable serving
changes, not new inference algorithms.

The remaining diagnostics describe the tested implementation. With the
stock MTP head, replacing argmax proposals with the tested sampled rule
offers little acceptance gain and no measured speed gain. In the profiled
EXL3 prefill, fp16 matrix multiplication dominates; the modest improvement
from a larger chunk does not rule out a different kernel or a separate
prefill representation. Recent disaggregated quantization explicitly
explores that alternative \citep{panferov2026disaggregated}. Neither result
justifies declaring a permanent limit of the model or format.

The longer math traces require more caution. We observe extra tokens and
re-verification markers, but the comparison changes artifact and engine
together. A shift toward repeated reasoning is a plausible interpretation,
not an isolated causal effect of quantization. The tested recalibration and
bit-allocation variants do not settle what another recipe, training method
or reference precision could achieve.

At a binding power cap, improving throughput tends to improve tokens per
GPU-board joule by a similar factor. This does not make tokens/J a quality
metric: a longer wrong answer can score well on throughput, and board
telemetry excludes host energy. Batching improves aggregate tokens/J here
while slowing individual replies. The choice between a single-user and a
shared configuration therefore depends on latency and concurrency needs,
not one universal efficiency ranking.

\section{Limitations and Claim Ceiling}
\label{sec:limits}

The accuracy results are observations on finite samples, not demonstrations
of equivalence. E47 tests 500 GSM8K and 500 MMLU items; the drafted point
estimates are slightly higher, but non-significant McNemar tests cannot
establish losslessness. E48 compares two bits with the higher-bit GGUF only
on 200 GSM8K items. Larger MMLU or MATH comparisons against that reference
were not run. The smaller temperature-1 evaluations use 20--200 items with
one sample per condition; one baseline repeat differs by three GSM8K
answers, which cannot define a general noise threshold. Small paired effects
remain uncertain \citep{hochlehnert2025sober,miller2024errorbars}, and similar
aggregate accuracy can conceal different wrong answers
\citep{dutta2024accuracy}.

All results concern one model, one GPU, one 80\,W cap and the named artifacts
and engines. The host carried other workloads. Timed prompt tests use only
two or three repeats, and the sustained energy comparison has one run per
lane at different durations. Several quality comparisons use earlier-day
baselines; one uses 50 rather than 100 GSM8K items. Mechanism probes cover
one profiled prefill or small trace sets. Energy is GPU-board energy, not
whole-system energy or energy per correct answer. Cross-engine comparisons
cannot isolate a quantizer's causal effect; nominal bit labels are not
matched effective precision. The faster resident two-bit run versus an
offloaded higher-bit reference does not establish an intrinsic engine ranking.

EXL3 experiments E7--E49 loaded system cuBLASLt and NVRTC 12.1 alongside
torch's bundled 12.8 libraries. Fully bundled controls retained the
nondeterminism (64/100 identical undrafted pairs versus 50/100 with mixed
libraries), and the drafted timing lay within the observed run-to-run
spread. These limited controls do not show that every earlier throughput
or quality result is unaffected; the main sustained runs were not repeated
under the corrected environment.

Most performance evidence uses exllamav3~1.4.7. Later controls test output
identity, not a complete performance rerun. By the September 28 literature
check, exllamav3~1.5.2 was available and TabbyAPI exposed the checkpoint
options studied here. New engines, draft heads and quantized checkpoints
are not ranked by this report. The recommended files preserve a measured
historical configuration, not a claim to the best current local model.
The three experimental recalibration/recipe weight sets were deleted;
their hashes and conversion recipes remain, but exact reruns require
regenerating them.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Direct deployment comparisons.}
MTP acceleration of Qwen3.8-27B is already documented. Ollman's
author-maintained RTX~3090 report measures EXL3 at 3.0\,bpw with decode
increasing from 43.6 to 94.8 tokens/s, alongside long-context fidelity
tests \citep{ollman2026qwen38}. Ang's first-party RTX~3090 study measures
GGUF drafting speed and GPU energy at about four bits, plus a separate
lower-bit quality and memory ladder; it explicitly leaves board-power
measurements below four bits untested \citep{ang2026qwen38}.
Tsai provides another desktop MTP benchmark \citep{tsai2026rtx3090spec}.
These are useful deployment reports, not independent replications of our
laptop tests. Their different GPUs, power, precision, workloads and
software prevent a normalized speed or quality ranking. Our contribution
is the paired low-bit evidence and diagnostic detail at the 12\,GB,
80\,W operating point, not the discovery that this model can self-draft.

\paragraph{Quantization and reasoning length.}
Longer reasoning under low-bit quantization is already reported for other
models and scalar quantizers
\citep{liu2025hurts,lian2026inflates,lotfi2026longer,alimaskina2026extreme}.
For this model, Kozyrev and Maiboroda report no inflation with a particular
four-bit NVFP4 intervention \citep{kozyrev2026gdn}, while a community GGUF
benchmark reports longer two-bit outputs \citep{migdal2026qwen38quants}.
The KTopt author model card also measures reasoning lengths for a roughly
3.06-bpw trellis GGUF with an optional correction adapter against Q8
\citep{wiklif2026ktopt}. Thus neither trellis quantization nor its effect on
length is an unmeasured category. Our 2.00-bpw EXL3 results describe a
different artifact, engine and task sample.

Lotfi et al.'s marker penalty motivates our decoding-time test
\citep{lotfi2026longer}. Self-generated calibration and task-aware
calibration are established approaches
\citep{williams2024selfcal,wang2026ayot,wang2026tasa}; our null results concern
specific changes to an already self-calibrated checkpoint. The bit-allocation
probe is consistent with distributed quantization damage
\citep{hu2026damage}, but does not prove its mechanism. Panferov et al.\ train
separate compute-native prefill weights and evaluate SSD-offloaded prefill
for Qwen3.8-27B \citep{panferov2026disaggregated}. That work is an alternative
to the pinned weight-only path profiled here, not something our chunk-size
experiment rules out.

\paragraph{Drafting and recurrent state.}
Exact speculative verification predates this report
\citep{leviathan2023fast,chen2023accelerating}; BEBOP analyzes MTP rejection
sampling with a head trained for that rule \citep{li2026bebop}. We test the
stock head and one verifier implementation, not their method under matched
conditions. DFlash supplies an alternative block drafter
\citep{chen2026dflash}. SpecLA, TreeWY and Bole reduce recurrent-state
verification costs through factor or replay designs
\citep{wang2026specla,ghantasala2026treewy,wang2026bole}, and GDN Tree-Scan
serves branch-local replay in vLLM \citep{ma2026gdntreescan}. Our slot
measurement concerns the snapshot design in the pinned deployment.
Recurrent-state precision is itself an active optimization target
\citep{zhang2026damp}; batching and speculation also interact
\citep{su2023synergy}.

\paragraph{Energy and reproducibility.}
Consumer-GPU energy and speculative-decoding energy are already benchmarked
\citep{zahl2026energy,dutta2026specenergy}. Ultra-low-bit work also measures
a 2B model on Intel laptop GPUs \citep{georganas2025ultralow}. Luo et al.\
profile this 27B model at BF16 under much higher workstation power caps and
show a substantial gap between GPU-board and whole-system energy
\citep{luo2026energy}. We report only board energy and do not compare those
figures as hardware efficiency rankings. Numerical nondeterminism is also
known \citep{yuan2025nondeterminism}; our contribution is a reproducible
engine-specific report and the bounded control tests of its upstream fix.

\section{Conclusion}

Self-drafting makes the tested two-bit Qwen3.8-27B deployment faster within
a 12\,GB, 80\,W laptop budget: EXL3 completion throughput rises from about 29 to
48.7--50.4 tokens/s on code and math and 35.7 on prose. Its sustained
GPU-board efficiency is 0.559 tokens/J, compared with 0.577 for the tested
drafted GGUF run. Reducing unused concurrency slots saves 2,176\,MiB, and
a denser checkpoint grid reduces one edited-prompt latency from 10.1 to
5.0\,s at a host-memory and cold-prefill cost.

The paired accuracy tests show no measured decrease with drafting on 1,000
greedy items, not equivalence or losslessness. Prefill profiling, acceptance
measurements and output-reproducibility controls explain limits of the
pinned implementation and identify changes worth testing. They do not
establish permanent architectural limits or rank newer engines. The usable
result is an auditable deployment baseline, practical configuration changes,
and a clear record of where additional comparisons are needed.
